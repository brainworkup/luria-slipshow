# Clinician guide · about 8 minutes

Keep the emphasis on understanding and practical support. Invite the parent or patient to interrupt. The presentation is a teaching companion, not a diagnostic tool.

| Scene | Time | Suggested narration / action |
|---|---:|---|
| Welcome | 0:25 | “Before we look at your results, let’s get comfortable with how scores work. They help us ask questions; they do not define you.” |
| Compared with whom? | 0:40 | Name the actual reference group and any adjustments used in the real report. Ask what the family hopes to understand. |
| Bell curve | 0:40 | “The height shows how common a score is. More people are around the middle, fewer toward the ends. ‘Normal’ is the name of the shape.” |
| Explorer | 1:15 | Start at the middle. Choose “One step below.” Trace the shaded area to the left. “This is about the 16th percentile.” Move above the middle, then return to center. |
| Percentiles | 0:40 | Distinguish rank from percentage correct. Open the comprehension check and invite an answer before revealing it. Percentiles do not form an equal-interval ability scale. |
| Score rulers / SD | 0:55 | Compare 85, 40, and 7: each is one SD below its scale's mean. “Different rulers, same position in this model.” If useful, show ±2 SD = SS 70–130, T 30–70, scaled 4–16, roughly 2nd–98th percentiles. |
| Ranges | 0:45 | “These labels help us organize a report, but the boundary is not a cliff.” Explain confidence intervals using the real test's reported interval; do not invent a universal error margin. |
| Rarity and functioning | 0:55 | “One unusual result is a clue. We look at the whole pattern and everyday life.” The number of measures affects how often a low score appears. Do not apply an adult study's base-rate percentage to children. |
| Domain drilldown | 1:00 | Select all three domains. Discuss questions the profile might raise without drawing a diagnosis. Bars represent rank, not twice-as-much memory or a significant difference. |
| Luria handoff | 0:30 | Ask what matches the person's experience. Confirm correct case and privacy, then switch to Luria. Start with the broad domain view and inspect related measures. |

Total: approximately 7 minutes 45 seconds, plus questions. For a five-minute version, shorten the mathematical detail and explore one domain. For ten minutes, invite the family to move the slider and discuss two everyday examples.

## Boundaries to preserve

- Scores are estimates of performance under particular conditions; they do not directly measure worth, effort, potential, or diagnosis.
- Test-specific norms, measurement error, confidence intervals, validity, language/cultural context, and clinical history govern actual interpretation.
- Average performance can coexist with real functional difficulty or decline from an earlier level. A low result can occur without disease.
- On symptom scales, higher may mean more concerns; do not reuse performance labels automatically.
- Validity measures need their own evidence-based interpretation, not the general bell-curve labels. Avoid treating them as simple effort or honesty meters.
- A difference between two scores is not automatically a statistically reliable or clinically meaningful difference. Check test-specific difference/base-rate evidence.
- Synthetic drilldown values are rank demonstrations, not composite scores or recommendations for a specific patient.

## Why this differs from the supplied broad plot

The original plot joins “abnormal/impaired” with statistical rarity and possible brain dysfunction. This presentation separates statistical description from clinical inference. That change is intentional: the deck leads to clinical interpretation rather than making the diagnosis from a cutoff.
